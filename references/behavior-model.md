# Behavior model: BMAP, empathy questions, Psych framework

Use when users aren't doing the behavior you expect and you need to understand why before changing anything.

## B = M·A·P

A behavior happens only when **Motivation**, **Ability**, and a **Prompt** converge at the same moment.

| Category | Lever | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation | 🎈 Anticipation | Seeking hope, avoiding fear |
| Motivation | 💚 Sensation | Seeking pleasure, avoiding pain |
| Motivation | 💛 Belonging | Seeking acceptance, avoiding rejection |
| Ability | ⏰ Time | How long does it take? |
| Ability | 💵 Money | How costly is it? |
| Ability | 💪 Physical capacity | How physically demanding is it? |
| Ability | 🧠 Mental capacity | How complicated is it? |
| Ability | 🔄 Practice | How familiar is it? |
| Prompt | 💥 Explicit prompt | The what-to-do-next is in the prompt itself (email, notification, button, timer) |
| Prompt | 🌀 Implicit prompt | Cued by association in memory (places, people, situations, emotions) |

Key rules:
- **Ability is the scarcest lever**: a user's ability at a moment is a function of whichever of the five ability resources is scarcest right then.
- **Never force a behavior.** Align the product with what users already want to accomplish, highlight how it helps them get there (Motivation), only then make it easy (Ability) and cue it clearly and timely (Prompt).
- Without a prompt, there is no action — validate that a clear nudge exists at the right moment.

## General Empathy Questions (GEQs)

Use in surveys or interviews to find Jobs-To-Be-Done and behavior levers. Get ~5 answers per question; one video call is often worth 100 survey answers.

1. 🌈 **Hope**: "If you had a magic wand and could instantly X, how would that change your life?"
2. 💀 **Pain**: "What's your #1 challenge when it comes to X? Why is it so challenging?"
3. 🚧 **Barrier**: "Tell me about the last time you did X — how did that go? What was preventing you from Y?"

Tip: ask for stories, not just answers — append "Be super specific. Tell us a story if possible to give us some context."

## Specific Empathy Questions (SEQs)

For one critical step (e.g. signup completion), ask both segments — a 2×2 of blockers and enablers:

| | Behavior blockers | Behavior enablers |
|---|---|---|
| **Successful customers** | 🐛 What made you hesitate the most before you [action]? | 🎉 What convinced you to [action]? |
| **Dropout customers** | 🚧 What made you hesitate to [action]? | 🔮 What would have made you more likely to [action]? |

SEQs surface deliberate System-2 reasoning; System-1 instinct problems are what the B.I.A.S. framework addresses.

## Psych framework

**Psych = Motivation − Friction** (net perceived value of each interaction). Treat the user as a game hero and psych as their health bar:

- Your job is to help them achieve *their* quest, not just business goals.
- Every element on a screen adds or subtracts psych. Motivating users matters as much as removing friction.
- If psych drops too low, it's game over — for them and for you.
- Friction isn't always bad: "good friction" that aligns with the user's motivations (e.g. a meaningful question during setup) can add net psych.
- Focus on insights, not numeric precision — the point is to simulate the user's mind while scanning the screen.

To apply: take a screenshot of the screen, annotate the user's likely reactions in scan order (informed by GEQ/SEQ research), and mark each with +/− psych. The biggest psych drops are your redesign targets.

## 6P Story

Sketch the customer's real-life story in 6 comic panels (5 words max per panel, stick figures fine). Write the happy ending (panel 6) first. The focus is the customer's life, not the product — psychology: narrative bias, singularity effect, character identification make stories the fastest route to shared empathy. Comics also force the brain to fill gaps between panels, which surfaces new questions and solutions.

**Worked example — Airbnb guest story** (note the product barely appears; it's the customer's day):

1. *Landing at destination* — "Whew… that was a long flight."
2. *Getting to Airbnb listing* — "Can't wait to arrive and unwind."
3. *Checking in* — "Hmm… where's that key lockbox again?"
4. *First tour of listing* — "Oooh, nice spot! I wonder where I could eat in the area."
5. *Exploring around* — "I love this neighborhood."
6. *First night* — "Time for bed! I'll visit some more tomorrow."

Each panel is a context + an emotion + a thought. Frictions (panel 3) and opportunities (panel 4's restaurant question) fall out of the story naturally.

For an Upheal analog, the "customer" is a provider moving through their clinical day (back-to-back sessions, notes piling up, insurance deadlines) — the product should appear only where it intersects that story.
